Twenty-something years of writing code, breaking production, and slowly learning how distributed systems actually behave (it is rarely what the docs promise).

Started on PHP and MySQL somewhere around 2004. Drifted through Python, Django, JavaScript, Go, Java, a bit of Ruby when it was cool. Got hooked on the parts of the stack that hurt — Kafka, NoSQL, multi-DC, the place where latency lives. Helped push a few companies from monoliths to microservices when that was still an opinion, not a default.

Right now I spend most days elbow-deep in LLMs — prompts, tools, agents, embeddings, the messy plumbing that turns a model into a product. Less interested in the hype, more interested in what survives contact with real users.

Along the way I have led teams big and small, but the part I actually enjoy is the technical problem in front of me. Architecture, weird performance bugs, the surprising state diagram nobody drew.
